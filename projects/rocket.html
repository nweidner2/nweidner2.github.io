Modular Deployable Rocket Payload

Senior Design · In Progress

Our senior design team is developing a high-powered rocket with a modular deployable payload system for the Ohio University Rocket Club. The project is currently in the early stages, focused on clarifying the need, defining requirements, understanding system scope, and exploring possible concepts.

This page is intentionally brief while the team is still defining the project. It will grow as concepts are selected and real CAD, prototypes, analysis, and test results become available.

01 · The Need

A rocket that can carry and deploy a useful payload

The current project direction is to design and build a rocket around a modular payload concept. The payload should be contained within the vehicle during ascent, deploy during flight, and support different payload types without requiring the entire system to be redesigned for each mission.

02 · Current Scope

Rocket + payload system

The team is responsible for both the rocket and the payload system. At this stage, the focus is understanding how the payload concept affects the rocket and translating customer expectations into clear system requirements before committing to a detailed design.

03 · What I'm Working On Now

Defining the problem before jumping into CAD

Project Framing

Clarifying the need statement, system boundary, scope, and deliverables.

Requirements

Turning customer, safety, flight, payload, and recovery needs into measurable design requirements.

Ideation

Beginning concept generation and considering how possible payload approaches interact with the overall rocket.

04 · Project in Progress

Outcome to come

The team has not yet selected a final deployment architecture or completed detailed design. As the project progresses, this case study will be updated with the selected concept, CAD, analysis, prototypes, ground testing, and final flight results.

Current takeaway: one of the most important steps so far has been defining the system boundary and requirements clearly enough that later design decisions can be defended.
